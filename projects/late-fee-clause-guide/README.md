# Late Fee Clause Guide — How to Use

You are a freelancer or small business owner. Before you send the first invoice, you want the contract or quote to already say: if payment is late, a fee applies. This guide shows you exactly where to put those clauses and what they should say.

## What is in this guide

1. **The clause text below** (English, ready to paste): late-fee clause, due-date clause, and payment-terms clause.
2. **This guide**: where each clause goes in a contract or quote, and two worked examples.

## Where to put the clauses

### In a contract (services agreement)

Add a section titled **"Payment Terms"** or **"Invoicing & Late Payment"** after the scope-of-work section. Use this structure:

1. **Due date** — "Invoices are due within 30 days of the invoice date."
2. **Late fee** — "A late fee of 1.5% per month (18% per year, simple, non-compounding) applies to any amount not paid by the due date."
3. **Suspension right** — "Work will be paused until overdue amounts are paid."

Paste the clause text from the guide into these three lines. Do not change the percentages unless you have discussed them with your client in writing.

### In a quote / proposal

Add a line at the bottom, before the signature block:

> "Payment terms: Net 30. Late payments accrue 1.5% per month (18% per year, simple, non-compounding). See attached contract for full terms."

### Why 18% per year?

- 1.5% per month (18% per year) is a commonly used rate in US freelance and small-business contracts. Check your local rules — some jurisdictions cap late-fee rates.
- It is simple, non-compounding: the fee is charged on the original overdue amount only.
- It is low enough to be defensible in a dispute, and high enough to actually change behavior.

## Two worked examples

**Example 1 — $1,000 invoice, 2 months late**

Fee = $1,000 × 1.5% × 2 = **$30**

The client owes $1,030. (The companion script prorates by day, so 60 days gives about $29.59.)

**Example 2 — $2,000 invoice, 3 months late**

Fee = $2,000 × 1.5% × 3 = **$90**

The client owes $2,090.

## Limitations

- This is a template, not legal advice. Check your local rules before using it in a real contract.

- The 1.5%/month rate is a starting point, not a recommendation for your specific situation.
